НАЗВАНИЕ:
Is Willpower Really a Battery? (What 59 Labs Found)

Запасные названия (A/B-тест):
  - Willpower Doesn't Run Out? The Famous Experiment Failed to Repeat
  - Why Scientists Can't Find the Willpower Battery

ОПИСАНИЕ (вставить как есть):
Everyone says willpower is like a battery that drains by evening. So why can't scientists find the battery? 🔋

The idea comes from a famous 1998 experiment: people who had to resist chocolate and eat radishes instead gave up sooner on an impossible puzzle. Hundreds of studies seemed to agree, and a 2010 review found a medium-sized effect.

But big, careful repeats found almost nothing. In 2016, 23 labs with 2,141 people found an effect close to zero. In 2021, 36 labs with 3,531 people again found a tiny effect close to zero. The only hint of an effect in the 2021 study showed up in people who already felt tired (an exploratory result).

My verdict: willpower is probably not a fuel tank that runs dry. But tiredness, boredom and stress still make self-control harder.

What to do (my advice, not a finding): rest first, make good choices easier (pack the gym bag the night before, hide the cookies), and take real breaks.

This video is educational and is not medical or psychological advice. Do you feel your willpower 'run out' by evening? Tell me below.

Sources:
- Baumeister, R. F., Bratslavsky, E., Muraven, M., & Tice, D. M. (1998). Ego depletion: Is the active self a limited resource? Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 74(5), 1252-1265.
- Hagger, M. S., et al. (2016). A multilab preregistered replication of the ego-depletion effect. Perspectives on Psychological Science, 11(4), 546-573. https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691616652873
- Vohs, K. D., et al. (2021). A multisite preregistered paradigmatic test of the ego-depletion effect. Psychological Science, 32(10), 1566-1581. https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797621989733

НАЗВАНИЕ:
Does 'Letting It Out' Really Help When You're Angry?

Запасные названия (A/B-тест):
  - Punching a Pillow Doesn't Calm You Down? What Science Found
  - Venting Anger: Steam Valve or Fire?

ОПИСАНИЕ (вставить как есть):
'Don't bottle it up. Let it out.' It sounds logical. But is anger really like steam in a pot? 🔥

The old idea is called catharsis: act out your anger and it drains away. In a 2002 experiment (Bushman), 600 college students wrote an essay and got insulting feedback. Then one group hit a punching bag while thinking about the person who insulted them, one group hit the bag while thinking about getting fit, and one group just sat quietly.

If catharsis were right, the group that vented the most should have calmed down the most. Instead, the group that hit the bag while thinking about the insulter ended up angriest, and was the most aggressive when later given a chance to blast that person with loud noise.

My reading: replaying the insult while venting isn't releasing anything, it's rehearsing. Important caveats: one lab study with students, and talking things through with a supportive friend is a different thing from punching a bag while you stew.

What to do (my advice, not a finding): let your body calm down first, take your mind off it, and talk it through once you're calmer.

This video is educational and is not medical or psychological advice. What do you do when you're really angry? Tell me below.

Sources:
- Bushman, B. J. (2002). Does venting anger feed or extinguish the flame? Catharsis, rumination, distraction, anger, and aggressive responding. Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 28(6), 724-731. https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167202289002

  6. Советы: не использовать как оскорбление; спрашивать «как я пойму, что неправ?»; проверять догадку тестом, числом или честным другом.

НАЗВАНИЕ:
Are Incompetent People Really Blind to It? (The Dunning-Kruger Myth)

Запасные названия (A/B-тест):
  - The Dunning-Kruger Effect Might Be Mostly Statistics
  - Why the Dunning-Kruger Meme Isn't the Whole Story

ОПИСАНИЕ (вставить как есть):
'The less you know, the more confident you are.' Everyone's heard it. So why do scientists still argue about it? 🧠

In 1999, Kruger and Dunning tested students on humor, grammar and logic and asked them to guess how they did. People in the bottom quarter scored around the 12th percentile but guessed around the 62nd.

But there's another way to get that picture. If everyone guesses their own rank badly, and most people feel above average, then the lowest scorers can only guess too high, and the top scorers can only guess too low. In 2020, researchers re-analyzed such data and concluded the effect is mostly a statistical artifact. A 2023 replication reported a small but statistically significant effect remains.

My verdict: overconfidence is real and everyone has it. The dramatic version, that unskilled people are blind to their own gaps, is probably exaggerated.

What to do (my advice, not a finding): don't use the label as an insult. Ask 'how would I know I'm wrong?' and check your guess against a test, a number, or an honest friend.

This video is educational and is not medical or psychological advice. When were you sure you'd nailed something... and hadn't? Tell me below.

Sources:
- Kruger, J., & Dunning, D. (1999). Unskilled and unaware of it: How difficulties in recognizing one's own incompetence lead to inflated self-assessments. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 77(6), 1121-1134.
- Gignac, G. E., & Zajenkowski, M. (2020). The Dunning-Kruger effect is (mostly) a statistical artefact: Valid approaches to testing the hypothesis with individual differences data. Intelligence, 80, 101449.
- Dunkel, C. S., Nedelec, J., & van der Linden, D. (2023). Reevaluating the Dunning-Kruger effect: A response to and replication of Gignac and Zajenkowski (2020). Intelligence, 96, 101717.

НАЗВАНИЕ:
Does Money Buy Happiness? Two Scientists Disagreed, Then Worked It Out

Запасные названия (A/B-тест):
  - Money and Happiness: The Plot Twist
  - Does Happiness Stop Rising After a Certain Income?

ОПИСАНИЕ (вставить как есть):
'Money can't buy happiness.' One famous study said more money stops helping at some point. Another said the opposite. Then the two scientists teamed up. 💰

A 2010 study (Kahneman & Deaton) found that day-to-day happiness flattened out at around $75,000 a year. A 2021 study (Killingsworth) pinged more than 33,000 workers on their phones, 1.7 million times in all, and found happiness kept rising with income, with no plateau.

In 2023, Killingsworth, Kahneman and a third scientist re-checked the data by how happy people were to begin with. For most people, happiness kept rising with income. For the least happy group (about 1 in 5), it rose and then flattened. So both studies were right, about different people.

Warnings: these are links, not proof that money causes happiness, and scientists still dispute exactly where the flattening point is, so no magic number.

My verdict: money helps most people, but it's not a cure for unhappiness that comes from somewhere else.

КРАТКАЯ ВЫЖИМКА (ход рассуждения в видео):
  1. Вопрос: работает ли «думай позитивно».
  2. Эксперимент 2009 (Wood и др.): повторять «я достойный любви человек». У людей с высокой самооценкой небольшой плюс; у людей с низкой настроение стало хуже, чем у тех, кто фразу не повторял.
  3. Рассуждение: если не веришь фразе, мозг спорит с ней, и позитивное предложение приглашает все негативные мысли.
  4. Исследование 2002 (Oettingen & Mayer): четыре группы (ищущие работу, влюблённые студенты, экзамен, операция на бедре). Кто ожидал успех, прилагал больше усилий и добивался больше; кто много фантазировал об успехе, прилагал меньше усилий и добивался меньше.
  5. Гипотеза авторов: яркая картинка успеха создаёт ощущение, что ты уже «приехал» (это предположение, не доказанный механизм).
  6. Вывод: зависит от вида. Вера в себя по реальным причинам помогает; фразы, в которые не веришь, могут навредить; мечтания без плана могут снижать усилия.
  7. Советы: правдоподобные слова («я учусь»), представлять и препятствие, составить маленький план: что, когда, где.

ПРОВЕРКА ФАКТОВ:
  Wood, Perunovic & Lee 2009 (Psych Science 20(7)); Oettingen & Mayer 2002 (JPSP 83(5)): ожидания → больше усилий, фантазии → меньше. Сравнение «ментального контрастирования» (позднее) не использовано как факт, а совет «представь препятствие» помечен как мой.

НАЗВАНИЕ:
Does Positive Thinking Actually Work? (It Can Backfire)

Запасные названия (A/B-тест):
  - Positive Affirmations Can Backfire. Here's When
  - Why Daydreaming About Success Might Hurt Your Chances

ОПИСАНИЕ (вставить как есть):
'Think positive and good things happen.' It sounds so reasonable. But what if, for some people, it backfires? ✨

In a 2009 study (Wood, Perunovic & Lee), people repeated 'I'm a lovable person.' Those with high self-esteem got a modest boost. But people with low self-esteem, the ones the advice is meant for, felt worse than those who didn't repeat it. One reason it could happen: if you don't believe the sentence, your mind argues back.

Then there's picturing success. In 2002, Oettingen and Mayer followed four groups (job seekers, students with a crush, students facing an exam, and hip-replacement patients). People who expected good results tended to put in more effort and do better. People who fantasized the most about success tended to put in less effort and do worse. The authors propose that vividly picturing success may make part of your mind feel as if you've already arrived (a proposal, not a proven mechanism).

My verdict: it depends on the kind. Believing you can, for real reasons, helps. Repeating sentences you don't believe can backfire, and daydreaming without a plan may drain your drive.

What to do (my advice, not a finding): use believable words ('I'm learning'), picture the obstacle too, and make a small plan (what, when, where).

This video is educational and is not medical or psychological advice. Do affirmations work for you, or do they make you roll your eyes? Tell me below.

Sources:
- Wood, J. V., Perunovic, W. Q. E., & Lee, J. W. (2009). Positive self-statements: Power for some, peril for others. Psychological Science, 20(7), 860-866.
- Oettingen, G., & Mayer, D. (2002). The motivating function of thinking about the future: Expectations versus fantasies. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 83(5), 1198-1212.
