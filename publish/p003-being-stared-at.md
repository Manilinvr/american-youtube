## Описание

```
You're on the train and suddenly you just know: someone is staring at you. 👀

You turn around... and they are! Is it a sixth sense? Scientists have been testing the feeling of being stared at for over a century (an early paper is from 1898), and careful tests have not found a reliable ability to sense unseen staring. A few experiments reported above-chance results, but a careful review suggested people could be learning patterns in the test sequences.

So why does it feel so real? A 2013 study (Mareschal, Calder & Clifford) showed people faces with blurry, hard-to-read eyes and asked where each face was looking. When the eyes were hard to read, people tended to see the gaze as aimed at them. The researchers describe it as an expectation that gaze is directed toward us, and suggest that assuming direct gaze may simply be a safer strategy. A direct gaze can also signal that an interaction, or a threat, is coming.

Caveat: the main experiments in that study used six observers, so it's a small lab study.

Next time you feel watched, remember: your brain is guessing, and it guesses "me".

This video is educational and is not medical or psychological advice. Have you ever turned around and caught someone looking? Tell me below.

Sources:
- Mareschal, I., Calder, A. J., & Clifford, C. W. G. (2013). Humans have an expectation that gaze is directed toward them. Current Biology, 23(8), 717-721. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2013.03.030
- Colwell, J., Schroder, S., & Sladen, D. (2000). The ability to detect unseen staring: A literature review and empirical tests. British Journal of Psychology, 91(1), 71-85.
- Titchener, E. B. (1898). The 'feeling of being stared at'. Science, 8(208), 895-897.
```

## Перевод для вас

Вы в поезде и вдруг «знаете», что на вас смотрят. Оборачиваетесь, и правда смотрят. Шестое чувство? Это проверяют больше ста лет (ранняя работа 1898 года), и аккуратные эксперименты не нашли надёжной способности чувствовать невидимый взгляд. В исследовании 2013 года (Mareschal, Calder, Clifford) людям показывали лица с размытыми, трудно читаемыми глазами и просили определить, куда смотрит лицо. Когда глаза трудно прочитать, люди склонны видеть взгляд, направленный на себя. Авторы называют это ожиданием прямого взгляда и предполагают, что так «безопаснее». Оговорка: в основных опытах было всего шесть наблюдателей.
