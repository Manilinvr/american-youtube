# p019 — The Case of the 38 Witnesses (Short, ~2:20, стиль «досье»)

**Title:** Did 38 People Really Watch and Do Nothing? | The Bystander Effect

**Description:**
In 1964 a newspaper said 38 people watched a woman be attacked and nobody helped. Psychologists built the "bystander effect" on it. Then researchers re-opened the case. Here's what the evidence actually says — and what to do if you ever see an emergency.

Sources:
- Manning, Levine & Collins (2007). The Kitty Genovese murder and the social psychology of helping: The parable of the 38 witnesses. American Psychologist 62(6), 555-562.
- Darley & Latané (1968). Bystander intervention in emergencies: Diffusion of responsibility. JPSP 8(4), 377-383.
- Fischer et al. (2011). The bystander-effect: A meta-analytic review. Psychological Bulletin 137(4), 517-537.
- Philpot et al. (2020). Would I be helped? Cross-national CCTV footage shows that intervention is the norm in public conflicts. American Psychologist 75(1), 66-75. doi:10.1037/amp0000469

The "my reading" and "my advice" parts are the Professor's own interpretation, not study findings.
